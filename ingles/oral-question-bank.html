C1 speaking question bank (ENAIRE Phase 3)

Use: answer out loud, 30-60 s per personal question and 60-90 s per opinion question. Record yourself, listen back, count filler words (muletillas). Basic structure: Point - Reason - Personal example - Nuance/counterargument - Closing. This is not an official ENAIRE bank; these are C1-style questions.

What is official (2025 bases, base 8, p. 12-13): an interview, in person or online, that measures comprehension, expression, fluency and communicative skills at C1 or above; 50 points, 25 to pass, eliminatory, and the mark counts in the final ranking (base 9, p. 14). ENAIRE publishes neither the parts nor the marking grid. In 2025, 532 of the 566 who sat it passed; the average was 40.1/50 and 33 people scored 50. The interviews were booked every 40 minutes in the afternoon of the same day as tests B and C. Section H turns an AI voice assistant into the examiner.

A. Personal (1-15)

  1. Tell me about yourself and what led you to apply to become an air traffic controller.
  2. What do you do in your free time, and why does it appeal to you?
  3. Describe your hometown. What would you change about it?
  4. How has your academic background shaped the way you think?
  5. What is the best piece of advice anyone has given you?
  6. Describe a moment when you felt really proud of something you achieved.
  7. Do you consider yourself a morning person? How does that affect your routine?
  8. What are you like under pressure? Give an example.
  9. Tell me about a person who has influenced you significantly.
  10. How do you usually deal with stress?
  11. What was the hardest thing you have ever had to learn?
  12. If you could live anywhere in the world, where would it be and why?
  13. How do you prefer to learn: alone, or with others?
  14. Describe a mistake you made and what you took from it.
  15. Where do you see yourself in ten years?

B. Opinion (16-30)

  1. Is it better to work for a large organisation or a small company?
  2. Some say that social media has made people less able to communicate face to face. To what extent do you agree?
  3. Should university education be free for everyone?
  4. Is it more important to be talented or hard-working?
  5. Do you think people rely too much on technology to make decisions?
  6. Is a good work-life balance realistic in demanding jobs?
  7. Should everyone learn to speak in public at school?
  8. How important is it to follow rules, even when they seem pointless?
  9. Are shift work and night work acceptable for essential public services?
  10. Is it better to specialise early or to keep a broad education?
  11. What makes a good team leader?
  12. Should companies be allowed to monitor their employees' performance with software?
  13. Is competition healthy in the workplace?
  14. Do you think stress is always harmful?
  15. How much should a person's salary depend on the risk and responsibility of the job?

C. Society, ethics, environment (31-48)

  1. What do you think are the biggest challenges facing Spanish society today?
  2. Should governments ban single-use plastics?
  3. Is it ethical to use artificial intelligence to make decisions that affect people's lives?
  4. Should air travel be taxed more heavily to reduce carbon emissions?
  5. How can cities become more sustainable?
  6. Is it right to reveal a colleague's mistake if it could put others at risk?
  7. Do individuals or governments bear more responsibility for climate change?
  8. Should short domestic flights be replaced by high-speed trains?
  9. What are the advantages and drawbacks of remote work?
  10. Is privacy more important than security?
  11. Should genetic engineering be used to prevent diseases? Where should we draw the line?
  12. How can we reduce the gap between rich and poor?
  13. Should older people be forced to retire at a certain age?
  14. Is tourism a benefit or a burden for cities like Valencia?
  15. Do you think whistleblowers should be legally protected?
  16. Should there be limits on how much time children spend on screens?
  17. Is it acceptable to lie in order to protect someone's feelings?
  18. What role should the media play in a democratic society?

D. ATC and aviation (49-60), general English, not phraseology (fraseología)

  1. Why do you want to be an air traffic controller rather than, say, a pilot?
  2. What qualities does a good air traffic controller need?
  3. Air traffic controllers must make quick decisions with incomplete information. How do you feel about that?
  4. What do you think is the most stressful aspect of the job?
  5. How would you cope with a mistake that could have had serious consequences?
  6. Controllers work in shifts, including nights and holidays. How would that affect your life?
  7. How might artificial intelligence and automation change air traffic control in the next twenty years?
  8. Why is English so important in international aviation?
  9. How can the aviation industry become more sustainable?
  10. What do you know about drones and how they might be integrated into airspace?
  11. Why is teamwork so important in a control room?
  12. Would you be willing to move anywhere in Spain for a posting? What would that mean personally?

E. 15 five-minute monologue topics

Structure for 5 min: opening (20 s) - 3 ideas x 75 s (idea, example, nuance) - counterpoint (40 s) - conclusion (25 s). Rehearse with a timer.

  1. A skill you would like to master and how you would go about it.
  2. The most important invention of the last century.
  3. Should air travel be reduced to fight climate change?
  4. A decision that changed the direction of your life.
  5. The role of failure in learning.
  6. How technology is changing the way we work.
  7. What makes a leader trustworthy in a crisis.
  8. Is Valencia a good model for a sustainable city?
  9. Being an introvert in a world that rewards extroverts.
  10. Should safety-critical jobs be automated as far as possible?
  11. A time you worked in a team and it did not go well.
  12. Is multilingualism an advantage or an overrated skill?
  13. How to make good decisions under pressure.
  14. The impact of tourism on local communities.
  15. What kind of legacy do you want to leave through your work?

F. C1 phrases and structures

Opening / buying time:

  • That's an interesting question. I suppose the first thing that comes to mind is...
  • Let me think about that for a moment.

  • It's not something I've given much thought to, but... Nuanced opinion:

  • I'm inclined to think that... / To my mind... / As far as I'm concerned...

  • I'd argue that... / It could be argued that...
  • To a certain extent I agree, although...

  • I'm somewhat torn, because on the one hand... on the other... Examples and generalizing:

  • A case in point is... / Take, for instance, ...

  • By and large... / On the whole... / In many respects... Contrasting and conceding:

  • That said, ... / Having said that, ... / Admittedly, ...

  • Whereas... / Despite the fact that... / Nonetheless... Cause and consequence:

  • This is largely due to... / As a result... / Consequently... / The upshot is... Hypotheses and advanced structures:

  • Were it up to me, I would... / Had I known, I would have...

  • Should the situation arise, I would...
  • Not only does it save time, but it also...
  • What matters most is... / It is the lack of X that worries me. (cleft sentences, oraciones hendidas)

  • The more you practise, the easier it gets. Rephrasing if you get stuck:

  • What I mean is... / Or, to put it another way... / How can I put this? Asking for clarification (interaction):

  • Could you elaborate on that? / Do you mean...? / Sorry, could you repeat the last part? Closing:

  • All things considered... / In a nutshell... / To sum up... Useful collocations: raise awareness, weigh up the pros and cons, strike a balance, take responsibility for, come to terms with, in the long run, a double-edged sword, bear in mind, deal with pressure, meet a deadline.

Typical mistakes of Spanish speakers to watch out for: "people is", "I am agree", "depends of", "actually" (= en realidad, not "actualmente"; a false friend, falso amigo), "affect/effect", not pronouncing -ed endings and short vowels (ship/sheep), the s líquida (s before a consonant; say "school", not "eschool"), preposition "in" vs "at".

G. Suggested practice method

  • Daily 15 min: 3 questions recorded on your phone. Weekly: one 5-minute monologue. Review with automatic transcription (transcription errors are often pronunciation problems).
  • Shadowing 10 min with BBC 6 Minute English or TED.
  • After 3-4 weeks, move from rehearsing alone to practising with humans (a Toastmasters club in English, an italki tutor).

H. AI examiner: ready-to-paste prompt

Use a voice assistant (for example a voice mode of ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude). Paste the block, then speak. Record the session yourself as well: the marks are only as good as the transcript the assistant hears. Use it once a week; change the topic number each time.

You are the examiner of an oral English interview for air traffic controller candidates in Spain
(ENAIRE, selection phase 3, test A). The official rules say only this: an interview that measures
comprehension, expression, fluency and communicative skills, guaranteeing at least CEFR level C1;
50 points, 25 to pass. Run a realistic 20-minute interview, then mark it.

INTERVIEW RULES
- Speak at natural native speed. One question at a time. Never help, never correct, never praise,
  never translate during the interview. If an answer runs past 90 seconds, interrupt politely and
  move on, as a real examiner would. If I ask for repetition, repeat once, rephrased.
- Ask follow-up questions that pick up something I actually said, including one that challenges
  my opinion ("Some people would say the opposite...").
- Structure, without announcing part names:
  1. Personal questions (4 min): 4 questions about my background, studies, work, free time and why
     I want to be an air traffic controller.
  2. Describe and speculate (4 min): describe in words a photograph of a busy workplace or public
     place; ask me what might be happening, how the people might feel and what could go wrong.
  3. Listening and summary (4 min): read aloud a 120-word opinion text on a current social topic,
     once; ask me to summarise it and to say whether I agree.
  4. Long turn and discussion (6 min): give me a topic (technology and jobs, climate and travel,
     responsibility at work, or education), 30 seconds to think, 2 minutes to speak, then 3
     follow-up questions that push me to abstract and hypothetical ground.
  5. Unexpected situation (2 min): a short role-play with a problem (for example a colleague
     disagrees with a safety decision, or a booking goes wrong) that I must resolve by talking.
- End with "Thank you, that is the end of the interview." Then wait for me to say MARK IT.

MARKING (only after I say MARK IT)
- Mark four criteria, 0 to 5 each, against CEFR: 5 = C2 qualities; 4 = solid C1; 3 = C1 with
  limits; 2 = B2; 1 = B1 or below.
  a) Comprehension: understood questions, the read text and the role-play twist first time?
  b) Expression: range and accuracy of grammar and vocabulary; pronunciation and intelligibility.
  c) Fluency: tempo, hesitation, length of turns, organisation and linking of ideas.
  d) Communicative skills: relevance, interaction, turn-taking, clarifying, persuading.
- For every criterion quote 2 or 3 things I said, word for word, as evidence, and give the
  corrected version of each error. Never invent a quotation; if you cannot recall my exact words,
  say so and lower your confidence.
- When in doubt between two bands, give the lower one.
- Then: total = sum of the four x 2.5, out of 50 (an unofficial conversion; ENAIRE does not
  publish its grid). Say whether the performance would be safely above 45, borderline, or below 25.
- Finish with my 5 most frequent errors, word for word with corrections, and ONE target for next
  week. No encouragement, no general advice.

Weekly record: date, topic numbers, the four marks, the total, the five errors. Ready when three sessions in a row on unseen topics give 45 or more with no criterion below 4 (self-assessment-rubric.md), and a human tutor or stranger agrees at least once.
