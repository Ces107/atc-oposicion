Vocabulary guide: how to learn the C1 and aviation English decks with Anki

What the exam covers

  • Phase 1, written English (test B): 20 multiple-choice questions at CEFR C1, maximum 20 points, pass mark 10 (2025 bases, base 8, p. 10). It feeds the ranking (base 8, p. 12; base 9, p. 14). In the 2025 call 1,544 of the 3,356 candidates who sat Phase 1 failed it (46 %), more than any other Phase 1 test. Every published score fits "+1 per right answer, -0.33 per wrong answer, 0 per blank" (worked out from the official Phase 1 list; the bases do not state the penalty). Only 12 people scored 20/20; the average of those who passed was 13.3.
  • What the real papers test: one sentence, one gap, four options. In the 60 real items we hold (2024 and 2025 calls) about 32 % are precise topic words (health, professions, clothes, cars, sport venues, farming, society), 20 % collocations and fixed phrases, and the rest grammar points that recur: possessive 's, inversion, rise/raise, causative have, so/neither, verb patterns. Not aviation English. Section 9 lists the points that recur.
  • Phase 3, oral English (test A): an interview, in person or online, scored out of 50, pass mark 25, also in the ranking (base 8, p. 12-13). See speaking/self-assessment-rubric.md.
  • ICAO requires at least Level 4 (Operational) on the licence after training; that is a hiring requirement, not part of the selection tests. See section 6.

2. Daily routine and Anki settings

  • Session length: 20 to 30 minutes, once a day, same time. Short daily beats long weekly. If you get absorbed and lose track of time, do at most 45 minutes on cards and switch to output (section 4): passive review beyond that has low return.
  • New cards per day: 15 (10 C1 + 5 aviation) in normal weeks; 25 in catch-up weeks. Never add new cards when your review backlog is above 150.
  • Reviews: do them all every day. The reviews are the learning; new cards are just the input.
  • Algorithm: if your Anki version has FSRS, enable it and set desired retention to 90%. Otherwise keep the default steps (1 min, 10 min) and set the graduating interval to 1 day.
  • Order: reviews first, then new cards, so the backlog never grows.
  • Audio: say each answer aloud (the oral interview is spoken). For phraseology cards say the full transmission out loud with the correct number pronunciation (tree, fife, niner).
  • Do not tag yourself "easy" too fast. Use Again when you needed more than 5 seconds or got the preposition/collocation wrong.

Phrasal verb Answer with meaning + one sentence of your own Learning only one meaning of a verb with several (take off, drop off)

Phraseology Say the readback exactly, in the same order as ATC gave it Answering "Roger" where a full readback is required
ICAO descriptors Compare adjacent levels (4 vs 5) and find the single word that differs (sometimes / rarely / almost never) Confusing which level is which

4. Personalized sentences (the step that makes it stick)

Once a week, take the 10 cards you failed most (Anki: Browse, "prop:lapses>1") and write one sentence per card about your own life: your preparation for ENAIRE, your city, your family, aviation. Rules:

  1. Real, specific and true: "I had to put off my mock exam because of a fever" beats a generic sentence.
  2. Use the target word in its correct pattern (preposition, collocation).
  3. Say it aloud, then paste it into the Back field under the answer (edit the card in Anki after import).
  4. Put 2 or 3 of the sentences together into a short paragraph as a writing exercise (5 lines, formal register with two linking devices).

5. Training protocol: from zero to a top mark

Calendar of the 2025 call, for planning: call published 20 Nov 2025, Phase 1 on 7-8 Feb 2026 (11 weeks later), Phase 2 on 13-19 Mar 2026, Phase 3 (oral English the same day as tests B and C) between 13 Apr and 29 May 2026. The written test comes first and early; the oral comes about ten weeks after it.

Scoring you train with: +1 right, -0.33 wrong, 0 blank (the 2025 scores fit exactly this rule). A blind guess is worth about 0; once you can rule out one option a guess is worth +0.11, two options +0.34. Rule: answer whenever you can rule out at least one option; leave it blank only when all four still look possible. No official time per test is published (the four Phase 1 tests together last "at least 4 h 30 min"), so train at 45 seconds per item: 15 minutes for 20.

Targets: written 18/20 or more after penalties (18.67 put you in the top 2 % of the 2025 sitters; 20/20 was 12 people). Oral 45/50 or more (166 of the 532 who passed in 2025 scored 45 or more; average 40.1).

Weeks Written test (per week) Oral (per week)

Error-log loop (uoe-error-log-template.md): every wrong or guessed item gets a row the same day (category, the rule in your own words, why you fell for it), a card the same day, a retest of the item 7 days later, and "Solid" only after three correct answers in different sets. Count the categories each week; the top two are next week's drill.

Target per practice set: first pass 21/25 or more after penalties on sets 01-12, 20/25 or more on sets 13-18 (harder words); every set redone after 3-4 weeks must reach 24/25.

Ready for the written test when all four hold: (1) two unseen replicas in a row at 18/20 or more after penalties, in 15 minutes or less; (2) both real papers redone after at least four weeks at 19/20 or more; (3) no error-log category with more than two open rows; (4) every word in section 9 known in both directions (English to Spanish and back).

Ready for the oral when: three full mocks in a row, on topics you did not prepare, marked 45/50 or more with no criterion below 4 of 5 (speaking/self-assessment-rubric.md), at least one of them with a person you do not know.

6. Using the ICAO rating scale as a self-assessment

The ICAO Language Proficiency Rating Scale has six levels (6 Expert, 5 Extended, 4 Operational, 3 Pre-operational, 2 Elementary, 1 Pre-elementary) and six criteria (pronunciation, structure, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension, interaction). The overall level is the lowest of the six ratings — one weak criterion caps the whole result. Once a fortnight record a 2-minute talk about an unexpected situation (a diversion, a medical emergency on board, a runway closure) and score yourself on the six descriptors on the cards. Note your weakest criterion and work on that one.

7. Traps and common mistakes

  • "Actually" is not "actualmente"; "eventually" is not "eventualmente"; "sensible" is not "sensible" (false friends deck).
  • "Advice", "information", "news", "evidence" are uncountable: no plural "s", no "an".
  • "Despite" + noun or -ing; "although" + clause.
  • On the radio: it is "affirm" and "negative", never "affirmative"; "say again", never "repeat"; "Roger" never replaces a readback of taxi, level, heading, speed, route, approach, runway or frequency instructions.
  • UK CAP 413 requires "degrees" after a heading ending in zero; the SKYbrary guide states this is not an ICAO requirement. Do not treat it as a general ICAO rule.

9. Highest-yield points of the real written test

From the 60 real items we hold (questions/english/uoe-real-dic2024.md, 40 items; uoe-real-nov2025.md, 20 items). Practice: sets 13-18 are built on this list.

How the items are built

  • Question 1 of both papers was a possessive with the noun left out ("at Maria's", "at a travel agent's"), and "All the above / None of the above are correct" was a wrong option both times.
  • Long sentences that describe a job and end in the gap want the exact profession (physician, midwife, seamstress).
  • Often three options mean the same and one means the opposite (affluent, wealthy, loaded / impoverished): the clue in the sentence decides.
  • British and American words both appear (tires, windshield, cast, pinkeye). Some real stems contain errors ("The police is"): choose the option that fits the gap.
